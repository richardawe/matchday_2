The idea: the Griot, West Africa's storyteller of battles, narrates every Premier League match as a clash between two warrior armies. Each big moment becomes a chapter card fans can share as it happens. At full time the chapters are cut into a 20-second reel with talking drums.

1 · The wow moment

The arena: what fans see when they arrive

The home page opens on a dark arena. The two warrior armies of the day's headline match (picked by the competition ranking we've just built) walk out of the shadows and face each other. Team-coloured light catches their edges, dust drifts and a Kente-patterned floor glows under them. The live score sits on a stone scoreboard between them and turns over when a goal goes in.

Scroll down and the arena hands over to the fixture pulse. With no live match, the arena shows the next big fixture with a countdown, or last night's reel.

How it's built: a three.js scene for phones and laptops that can handle it, loaded after the page has appeared. Lighter phones get a GSAP version: the same layers sliding in 2.5D, about 30 KB. Fans who prefer reduced motion get a still image. The statues are cut out of their black background once, automatically, so light and dust can sit behind and in front of them.

2 · During the match

Match chapters: story cards made automatically

Every kick-off, goal, red card, half-time and final whistle produces a card within seconds of our live feed seeing it. Cards appear on the match page as a running "story so far", become the link preview when someone shares the match, and come in WhatsApp Status, Instagram and X sizes. Each one is captioned by the Griot in the voice the fan picks.

Made automatically about 3 to 6 minutes after the final whistle, in 9:16 (WhatsApp Status, Reels, TikTok, Shorts) and 1:1 (feeds). Twenty seconds fits all of them. A quiet match with no goals still gets a reel: the beats switch to chances, saves and the stats.

Sound in version 1 is generated by us (talking drums, a crowd swell and a horn), so there's no music licence to worry about. Version 2 adds the Griot's voice.

4 · The African twist

Made to feel like home

The Griot narrates

The griot is West Africa's keeper of stories and battle songs. Every caption, card and reel is told in that voice: a battle, a hero, a fall, a verdict.

"Sing it, griot: the Legion held the wall."

Four voices to pick from

English, Naija Pidgin, Sheng / Swahili and Mzansi slang. Fans pick once and every card and caption follows.

"Goal don enter!" · "Bao la Saka!" · "Eish, what a strike!"

Talking drums, not stock music

Goals land on a talking-drum hit; the reel opens with a horn. All generated by us, so no music licence needed.

Patterns from home

Kente-style stripe bands in each team's colours frame every card, with geometric borders like the beadwork on the Zulu shield. These are our own patterns, not copies of a specific cloth.

Our time zones

Kick-offs in WAT, EAT or SAST based on where the fan is, not London time.

WhatsApp first

Every card and reel comes sized and compressed for WhatsApp Status, with one-tap share. That's where match banter lives.

African players in the story

When an African player scores, assists or gets sent off, the Griot says so: "Our son from Accra…", pulling from the African players feature the site already has.

Viewing-centre mode

A full-screen, TV-friendly live board for the viewing centres and bars where many fans watch: big score, live chapters, today's other games.

5 · How it works

From live event to card to reel

Live feed spots it

The ESPN live sync we just built sees a new goal, card or full time (every 20 seconds).

Card drawn on our server

PHP composes the card from the statue, the score and the Griot's caption in about a second. Works on your current cPanel hosting.

Shown and shareable

The card appears on the match page, becomes the link preview, and goes into the X and WhatsApp share tools.

Full time starts the reel

The server asks GitHub Actions to render the 20-second reel (cPanel can't render video). It takes 3–6 minutes.

Reel comes back

The finished MP4 is uploaded to the site, shown on the match page and home page, and queued for X.

7 · Rights and guardrails

Staying on the right side

Art: only the 20 rights-safe statues. No club crests, no Premier League logo, no player photos, no match footage. Club and player names, scores and minutes are facts we can state.

Data: the live data still comes from ESPN's unofficial feed. Turning it into public videos makes our use of it more visible, so the switch-off and the fallback we built still matter.

Words: captions come from templates first (the site's chronicle feature already writes match stories). An AI rewrite can add flavour, but every name, minute and score comes from the data, never from the AI.

Speed: the home page stays fast on mobile data. three.js loads only on capable devices, after the page has appeared, and never with Data Saver on.

CHECK BEFORE LAUNCH

Some statues are themed on club identity (for example Wolves' "Wolf Guard"). They're crest-free, but club colours plus names in automated posts can still draw attention from rights holders. I'm not a lawyer; a quick review before automatic posting starts is worth it.
